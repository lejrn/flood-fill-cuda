# Passenger elevator: make the hoist safe to ride, then fast, then easy on the motor, then steady and precise

**Chain at a glance:** Rope-and-pulley freight hoist -> Otis safety-catch hoist -> Steam-engine hoist -> Electric-motor hoist -> Counterweighted electric hoist -> Guided car with floor buttons

**The simple problem:** Loads must go up between factory floors; a rope hoist does it, but nobody may ride, because a snapped rope kills. [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Rope-and-pulley hoist [00:00]
- **Idea:** Haul the load up on ropes over pulleys.
- **Why it works:** A pulley turns a pull from below into lift.
- **But:** If the rope snaps, everything falls; riding is forbidden. [00:06] - `safety`
- **So:** Otis (1852): teeth that bite into the rail the instant the rope breaks. [00:12] - `fail-safe`
- **Shown as:** A crate swings from ropes in a toy factory; a worker figure lies sprawled below.
![Rope hoist, worker on the floor](frames/elevator/f003_0-00-11.jpg)

### 2. Otis safety hoist [00:12]
- **Idea:** A car on a rope with a catch beside a toothed rail.
- **Why it works:** Lose the rope's pull and the teeth lock into the rail.
- **But:** With passengers, the old hand-hauling is too slow. [00:25] - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Let an engine do the lifting. [00:32] - `automate`
- **Shown as:** Close-up of a catch arm swinging into a sawtooth rail; then a worker hand-cranking a rope drum.
![Catch biting the toothed rail](frames/elevator/f006_0-00-20.jpg)

### 3. Steam-engine hoist [00:32]
- **Idea:** A steam engine drives the hoist.
- **Why it works:** Steady power no worker can match.
- **But:** Every building needs its own boiler, and it is still slow. - `baggage`
- **So:** Siemens (1880) fits an electric motor. [00:36] - `substitute-medium`
- **Shown as:** A beam steam engine at the foot of the frame, the car creeping above.
![Steam engine at the hoist](frames/elevator/f009_0-00-32.jpg)

### 4. Electric-motor hoist [00:36]
- **Idea:** A motor at the top winds the car up.
- **Why it works:** Power by wire, no boiler.
- **But:** Lifting the heavy car again and again burns the motor out. [00:42] - `wear-or-leak`
- **So:** Hang a counterweight on the other side. [00:47] - `balance`
- **Shown as:** Close-up of a blue geared motor by a pulley wheel; the burn-out is only narrated.
![Electric motor](frames/elevator/f011_0-00-41.jpg)

### 5. Counterweighted hoist [00:47]
- **Idea:** A weight descends as the car rises.
- **Why it works:** It balances the car, "cuts the motor's load in half". [00:52]
- **But:** (implied) The car can sway and cannot stop precisely; named only as reasons for the last additions. - `side-effect`
- **So:** Guide rails on four sides, plus a floor button. [00:55] - `constrain`
- **Shown as:** Plates ride one side, the car the other, on one rope over the top pulley.
![Counterweight and car](frames/elevator/f014_0-00-55.jpg)

### Final: Modern passenger elevator [00:55]
- **Idea:** Enclosed car between guide rails, with safety catch, motor, counterweight and buttons.
- **Why it solves the chain:** Every fix stays: catch for the rope, motor for speed and boiler, counterweight for burn-out, rails and button for sway and stopping.
- **Reveal:** No name spoken; "the elevator as we know it was born" [01:02] over a pull-back to the full rig.
![Guide rails and button panel](frames/elevator/f015_0-00-58.jpg)

## Accuracy notes
- The first passenger elevator (1857) was already steam-driven; hydraulic elevators, the main step before electric, are skipped.
- Guide rails existed from the start (the catch grips one at 00:20) and counterweights predate electric motors; a counterweight is usually the car plus 40-50 % of rated load, so "half" is loose.
- Siemens's 1880 car climbed a toothed rack, motor underneath; push-buttons later replaced human operators.
