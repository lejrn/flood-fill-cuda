# Maglev train: a one-off magnetic shove is repeated along a track, made switchable, then timed so it never stops

**Chain at a glance:** Two magnets repel apart -> Track lined with magnets -> Electromagnet coils along track -> Current flipped at set points

**The simple problem:** Two magnets with like poles facing shove each other away, but it is a single shove: they coast and stop. How do you keep something moving using magnetism alone? [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Two repelling magnets [00:00]
- **Idea:** Put two bar magnets like-pole to like-pole and let them fly apart.
- **Why it works:** Like poles repel, so magnetism alone produces motion.
- **But:** The push only acts at close range; once apart, nothing keeps pushing and they stop [00:07]. - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Repeat the magnet along the whole route so there is always a neighbour pushing [00:07]. - `scale-up`
- **Shown as:** Red/blue bar magnets on a dark slatted desk snap apart and slide to a halt.
![Two magnets shooting apart](frames/maglev/f002_0-00-06.jpg)

### 2. Track lined with permanent magnets [00:07]
- **Idea:** Rows of same-polarity magnets along the path keep repelling the moving magnet ahead [00:16]; more magnets underneath lift it off the ground, so there are no wheels and no friction [00:22].
- **Why it works:** Every track magnet adds another push, and repulsion from below replaces the wheels.
- **But:** A permanent magnet's poles are fixed and cannot be flipped [00:26]. Why that matters is left implied (a fixed magnet ahead resists as much as the one behind pushes). - `control`
- **So:** Swap them for coils of wire; reversing the current flips the poles instantly, with nothing physically turning over [00:32]. - `field-not-motion`
- **Shown as:** Two long rows of magnets line a lane; then a long red-blue "train" magnet hovers over a guideway painted half red, half blue.
![Magnet-lined guideway with lift](frames/maglev/f005_0-00-23.jpg)

### 3. Electromagnet coils along the track [00:32]
- **Idea:** Coils installed all along the track [00:44]; those ahead pull the train and those behind push it, giving strong thrust [00:50].
- **Why it works:** Pull from the front and push from the back add together.
- **But:** Once the train passes a section, that section's force disappears [00:50]. - `control`
- **So:** Reverse the current every time the train reaches a set point [00:56]. - `sense-and-react`
- **Shown as:** A wire coil wraps a bar magnet with yellow leads; copper springs beside the guideway stand in for coils, white arrows mark pull and push, and the arrows swing to point backward once the train has passed.
![Coils pulling and pushing, shown with arrows](frames/maglev/f010_0-00-50.jpg)

### Final: Maglev train [00:56]
- **Idea:** The same coils, but the current flips whenever the train reaches a set point, so the coils in front always pull and those behind always push [00:56].
- **Why it solves the chain:** The track supplies a push everywhere (rung 1), the poles can change (rung 2), and the timing changes them at the right moment (rung 3), so propulsion never stops [01:02] while the magnets below keep the train floating.
- **Reveal:** Named only in the closing line, "the principle behind a maglev train" [01:08], as the camera pulls back to the smiling engineer seated behind a long toy track on the desk.
![Continuous propulsion along the guideway](frames/maglev/f015_0-01-08.jpg)

## Accuracy notes
- A row of fixed same-pole magnets would not give continuous thrust (the magnet ahead repels as much as the one behind), and permanent magnets alone cannot levitate stably (Earnshaw's theorem). Real systems use gap-controlled electromagnets (EMS: Transrapid, Shanghai) or superconducting magnets over induced currents (EDS: Japan's SCMaglev).
- Real propulsion is a linear synchronous motor: guideway coils fed alternating current synchronised with the train's position, forming a travelling magnetic wave. "Reverse the current at a set point" is a one-coil simplification.
- The ladder is a logical reconstruction, not history (e.g. Kemper's 1934 patent is not shown).
