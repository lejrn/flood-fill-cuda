# Solid rocket motor: tame a one-instant blast into a long, front-loaded, aimed push

**Chain at a glance:** Explosive shell with bottom hole -> Smaller explosive charge -> Solid slow-burning powder -> Powder with central hole -> Star-shaped hollow core -> Big star-core rocket with nozzle

**The simple problem:** An explosion inside a shell destroys it. Could the blast instead be vented out the back to fly the shell to space? [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Explosive shell with a bottom hole [00:04]
- **Idea:** Vent the blast downward through a hole in the base.
- **Why it works:** Gas pushed out one way pushes the body the other way.
- **But:** The explosion is too violent; the shell still bursts [00:10], and a bigger hole doesn't help [00:15]. - `doesn't-work`
- **So:** Use less explosive. - `other:reduce`
- **Shown as:** Cutaway of the vented shell, then a fiery burst that tears it apart.
![Vented shell bursts](frames/rocket-motor/f004_0-00-15.jpg)

### 2. Smaller charge [00:17]
- **Idea:** Shrink the charge so the casing survives.
- **Why it works:** Less energy, so the shell holds.
- **But:** All the push comes in one kick; it hops once and falls back [00:20]. - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Swap the explosive for slow-burning gunpowder. - `substitute-medium`
- **Shown as:** The shell pops briefly into the air and drops.
![One hop, then it falls](frames/rocket-motor/f006_0-00-22.jpg)

### 3. Solid slow-burning powder [00:23]
- **Idea:** Powder that burns from its end, little by little.
- **Why it works:** A burn gives a lasting push instead of a blow.
- **But:** Only the small end face burns, so the force is too weak to lift off [00:27]. - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Cut a hole through the centre to burn more powder at once [00:31]. - `reshape`
- **Shown as:** Cutaway of a grainy powder column glowing red only at its base.
![Powder burns only at its end](frames/rocket-motor/f008_0-00-28.jpg)

### 4. Central hole [00:31]
- **Idea:** A hollow core burns along its whole length.
- **Why it works:** More burning surface = more gas = more thrust, and it keeps growing as the hole widens [00:36].
- **But:** Thrust peaks late, while the shell needs the most force at liftoff [00:41]. - `control`
- **So:** Make the core star-shaped. - `reshape`
- **Shown as:** A see-through shell with a rod-like core, a widening spark jet, then a feeble flame at liftoff.
![Hole widens, thrust grows](frames/rocket-motor/f010_0-00-39.jpg)

### 5. Star-shaped core [00:41]
- **Idea:** A star cross-section for the hollow core.
- **Why it works:** The grooves give lots of surface first, so thrust is strong at liftoff [00:47], then fades as the star burns round [00:51].
- **But:** Gas leaving the hole spreads in every direction, scattering its force [00:56]. - `consumable-waste`
- **So:** Attach a cone to focus the gas downward. - `constrain`
- **Shown as:** A glowing orange star seen from below that rounds into a blob as it burns.
![Star-shaped burning core](frames/rocket-motor/f012_0-00-46.jpg)

### Final: Big star-core rocket with cone nozzle [01:01]
- **Idea:** Star core plus cone, scaled up with a bigger body and more powder [01:05].
- **Why it solves the chain:** Burning instead of exploding fixes 1-2; the hollow star gives enough, front-loaded thrust (3-4); the cone stops scattering (5); size supplies the total push.
- **Reveal:** "Rocket" is never said; the shell could "finally climb all the way to space" over a plume rising into blue sky.
![Plume climbing to space](frames/rocket-motor/f018_0-01-10.jpg)

## Accuracy notes
- Explosives detonate; propellants only burn fast. No hole or charge size turns a detonation into thrust. Gunpowder rockets also predate explosive shells, so this is a thought experiment, not history.
- Real names: end-burning, core-burning (progressive) and star grains; star grains usually aim at fairly flat thrust. The "cone" is a converging-diverging (de Laval) nozzle that accelerates exhaust to supersonic speed, not just a funnel.
- One solid motor can reach space (sounding rockets); orbit needs far more speed and staging.
