# Ondol: smoke goes from nuisance to heat source - led out, routed under the floor, spread, balanced, then pulled through by a chimney

**Chain at a glance:** Fire and hearth indoors -> Passage leads smoke out -> Widened warm passage -> Fire outside, smoke underfloor -> Several underfloor channels -> Thick-to-thin stone floor -> Tall chimney pulls flow

**The simple problem:** People need fire at home to cook and keep warm, but it fills the house with smoke. [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Fire and hearth indoors [00:00]
- **Idea:** Cook over an indoor fire: stones under a clay pot [00:03], then a bigger stone-and-clay hearth for iron pots [00:06].
- **Why it works:** Heat right where people live.
- **But:** Smoke fills the whole house [00:10] - `side-effect`
- **So:** Build a passage to let the smoke out [00:13] - `constrain`
- **Shown as:** Grey figures by a campfire, then a stone hearth; as smoke billows, a figure covers its face. (Three hearth versions, no flaw between them.)
![Figure covering its face in the smoke](frames/heated-floor-ondol/f004_0-00-12.jpg)

### 2. Smoke passage [00:13]
- **Idea:** A low stone duct carries smoke through the wall.
- **Why it works:** Smoke follows the channel instead of spreading.
- **But:** The passage grows warm [00:16]: heat leaves with the smoke (told as a discovery) - `consumable-waste`
- **So:** Widen the passage and use it to stay warm in winter [00:19] - `close-the-loop`
- **Shown as:** A stone duct runs under the wall; smoke drifts out the far side.
![Passage leading smoke out under the wall](frames/heated-floor-ondol/f005_0-00-16.jpg)

### 3. Widened warm passage [00:19]
- **Idea:** Broad stone slabs over the flue beside the hearth.
- **Why it works:** Smoke heats the stone; stone holds and releases heat.
- **But:** The fire is still indoors: smoke and sparks get in [00:23] - `side-effect`
- **So:** Move the fire outside the room; only smoke passes under the floor [00:26] - `split-functions`
- **Shown as:** A path of grey slabs from the hearth, then the fire mouth throwing sparks.
![Widened slab passage beside the hearth](frames/heated-floor-ondol/f006_0-00-20.jpg)

### 4. Fire outside, smoke underfloor [00:26]
- **Idea:** Fire pit outside the wall; its flue runs under the room.
- **Why it works:** The room gets the heat without smoke or sparks.
- **But:** (implied) one passage warms only a strip of floor - `control`
- **So:** Split the passage into several channels [00:31] - `reshape`
- **Shown as:** The pot-topped hearth now outside a stone wall, one line of stones running into the room.
![Fire pit moved outside the wall](frames/heated-floor-ondol/f008_0-00-28.jpg)

### 5. Several underfloor channels [00:31]
- **Idea:** Parallel channels spread smoke under the whole floor.
- **Why it works:** Hot smoke runs beneath every part of the floor.
- **But:** Too hot near the fire pit, too cold at the far end [00:35] - `control`
- **So:** Thick stones near the pit, thin ones farther away [00:39] - `reshape`
- **Shown as:** Rows of stone supports across the room, then smoke hanging thick in the channels nearest the fire.
![Passage split into channels](frames/heated-floor-ondol/f009_0-00-32.jpg)

### 6. Thick-to-thin stone floor [00:39]
- **Idea:** Floor stones graded by thickness along the smoke path.
- **Why it works:** Thick stone soaks up the strong heat near the fire; thin stone passes on the weaker heat far away.
- **But:** "The biggest problem": in longer passages smoke can't escape and is pushed back into the room [00:46] - `doesn't-scale`
- **So:** Smoke pushes up strongly but sideways weakly [00:51], so build a tall chimney at the end [00:56] - `self-powered`
- **Shown as:** Cutaway of thick and thin slabs, the finished flagstone floor, then long channels with smoke spilling out.
![Thick and thin floor stones](frames/heated-floor-ondol/f011_0-00-42.jpg)

### Final: Chimney-drawn heated floor [00:56]
- **Idea:** Rising smoke in the chimney leaves space that pulls the smoke behind it, sucking the whole flow through [00:58].
- **Why it solves the chain:** The smoke's own lift becomes suction along every channel; fire stays outside, channels cover the floor, graded stones even the heat.
- **Reveal:** Generic, never "ondol": "that's how the heated floor system was born" [01:05]; pull-back to the room model on the desk, figures lying on the warm floor, the engineer watching.
![Finished room model on the desk with chimney](frames/heated-floor-ondol/f018_0-01-10.jpg)

## Accuracy notes
- This is the Korean ondol, unnamed in the video: firebox (agungi), flues (gorae), floor stones (gudeul), chimney (gulttuk). One kitchen fire both cooks and heats, as the pot kept on the outside hearth hints.
- Chimney physics is loose: a tall column of hot, light gas weighs less than outside air, and that pressure difference (stack effect) draws the flow; taller means stronger draft.
- History is tidied: for centuries only part of the floor was heated; full-floor ondol spread much later.
