# APCBC: blunt for impact, pointed for flight, and a cap that breaks away at the moment the two needs conflict

**Chain at a glance:** Sharp-pointed shell -> Blunt-nosed shell -> Blunt shell under breakaway cap

**The simple problem:** A shell has to get through armor, even when it strikes the plate at a slant. [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Sharp-pointed shell [00:00]
- **Idea:** The intuitive answer the hook hands the viewer: a sharp tip to pierce.
- **Why it works:** A point concentrates force on a tiny spot (implied; the video presents it only as the viewer's assumption).
- **But:** Hitting armor at an angle, a pointed shell can glance off (ricochet) [00:13]. A stone analogy prepares this: a flat stone skips on water, a blunt one plunges in [00:08]. - `doesn't-work`
- **So:** Make the nose blunt so it bites. - `reshape`
- **Shown as:** A close-up of a pointed shell flying over desert sand at "Likewise"; the ricochet itself is not visible in the sampled frames.
![Pointed shell in flight](frames/armor-piercing-shell/f003_0-00-14.jpg)

### 2. Blunt-nosed shell [00:06]
- **Idea:** "In fact, it's the opposite" [00:04]: the real nose is blunt.
- **Why it works:** A blunt nose bites into the armor instead of sliding off [00:17].
- **But:** It produces enormous drag in flight [00:22]. - `side-effect`
- **So:** Wrap it in a streamlined cap: pointed while flying, blunt at impact. - `split-functions`
- **Shown as:** The blunt shell flies low over the sand, then a close-up of the blunt nose lands on "another problem" [00:20].
![Blunt nose at "another problem"](frames/armor-piercing-shell/f005_0-00-22.jpg)

### Final: APCBC [00:26]
- **Idea:** A streamlined ballistic cap over the blunt nose; on impact the cap shatters, exposing the blunt nose, which penetrates and then explodes inside [00:29].
- **Why it solves the chain:** Each shape exists only when it is needed: the cap handles flight (rung 2's drag) and the bare blunt nose handles impact (rung 1's ricochet).
- **Reveal:** The full name and acronym are spoken as the last line [00:37], "armor-piercing capped ballistic capped shell, APCBC", over a tank with the caption "capped shell".
![Streamlined cap with airflow streaks](frames/armor-piercing-shell/f006_0-00-26.jpg)

## Accuracy notes
- Historically the blunt part was a separate soft-steel penetrating cap (Makarov cap, 1890s) fitted over a still-pointed hard shell (APC), mainly to stop the tip shattering on face-hardened armor and to reduce ricochet; adding a thin windshield over that cap gave APCBC. The video folds the cap into the shell body and skips the shattering problem.
- The ballistic cap is a thin hollow shell that crushes on contact. Only APCBC-HE types carry a bursting charge with a base fuse; many APCBC rounds were solid shot.
- The stone analogy is loose: skipping depends mostly on a flat shape and a shallow angle.
