# Multi-stage steam turbine: catch the steam's leftover energy again and again

**Chain at a glance:** Breath on a pinwheel -> Steam jet, one blade row -> Fixed blades redirect steam -> Many stages plus generator

**The simple problem:** Blowing on a pinwheel spins it; can a blast of hot steam do the same, and can all its energy become rotation? [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Pinwheel blown by breath [00:00]
- **Idea:** Blow air at a pinwheel.
- **Why it works:** Moving air pushes angled blades, so the wheel turns.
- **But:** A breath is a weak, human-made push (implied; the video just asks what hot steam would do [00:04]). - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Swap breath for steam from boiling water. - `substitute-medium`
- **Shown as:** A blue-haired character with round glasses blows at a metal pinwheel at desk level.
![Blowing on a pinwheel](frames/steam-turbine/f001_0-00-03.jpg)

### 2. Simple single-row steam turbine [00:10]
- **Idea:** A steam jet hits one blade row on a shaft; the blades "spin the same way" [00:07].
- **Why it works:** Same push as air, but steam carries far more energy.
- **But:** After leaving the blades, the steam still has a lot of energy left [00:14]. - `consumable-waste`
- **So:** Fixed blades turn the escaping steam onto a second rotating row [00:19]. - `close-the-loop`
- **Shown as:** A flask on a burner jets steam at the pinwheel, then a nozzle sprays a small blade wheel on a rod above the desk, steam streaming past.
![Single blade row in a steam jet](frames/steam-turbine/f004_0-00-12.jpg)

### 3. Fixed blades plus rotating blades [00:19]
- **Idea:** A stationary row bends the used steam into the next moving row; it is "redirected and used again" [00:26].
- **Why it works:** Changing the flow's direction lets a second row harvest what the first missed.
- **But:** One extra pair still leaves energy in the steam (implied by the move to repetition [00:43]). - `consumable-waste`
- **So:** Stack many fixed/moving pairs along the shaft. - `combine-layer`
- **Shown as:** Two blade rows with white arrows tracing the steam's bend from the fixed row into the moving one.
![Fixed blade row redirects steam](frames/steam-turbine/f006_0-00-21.jpg)

### Final: Multi-stage steam turbine with generator [00:30]
- **Idea:** Steam strikes a stationary row, turns, pushes the rotating row with an "upward force" [00:36], and repeats through many rows [00:43]; the shaft drives a generator where coils and magnets move past each other [00:55].
- **Why it solves the chain:** Each stage recovers part of what the last one let escape, draining the steam step by step; the generator closes the last gap, that rotation is not yet electricity.
- **Reveal:** "This is the principle of a steam turbine" [00:49], then a question bolts on the generator.
![Many stages in cross-section](frames/steam-turbine/f012_0-00-45.jpg)

## How the video tells it
- **Hook (first ~5 s):** Everyday experiment (blow on a pinwheel), then "what happens if" with hot steam.
- **Storyteller:** Impersonal history ("At first, turbines were made..."); But / So, then "By repeating" and "Then how..." for the generator. No thinking-engineer moment.
- **Visual devices:** Desk-scale props (flask on a burner, tiny blade wheels on a rod); white steam and arrows for flow; then a switch to a monochrome blade cross-section with smoky flow, a real rotor cutaway, and a blue-tinted industrial generator cutaway. No red/blue heat coding; the characters are blue-haired figures, not the blue-glasses engineer.
- **Pacing:** 4 rungs in 01:05, about 16 s per rung, but back-loaded: rungs 1-3 take 30 s, the final 35 s.

## Accuracy notes
- Real names: a fixed row is a nozzle or stator row; each fixed+moving pair is a "stage". Stages exist mainly to drop the steam's pressure a little at a time (blades lengthen as it expands); the video frames it only as reusing leftover speed.
- History is compressed: de Laval's single-stage wheel and Parsons' multi-stage turbine both date from the 1880s. "Used again" is not a loop: the same steam flows on through each row, then exits to the condenser.
