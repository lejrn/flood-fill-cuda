# Ceramic disc faucet: every soft seal wears out, so remove the soft part

**Chain at a glance:** Rubber washer pressed shut -> Rotating ball with hole -> Mixing ball, one lever -> Ball sealed by seats -> Two flat ceramic discs

**The simple problem:** A tap must shut off water and keep doing so after thousands of closings; the first answer drips within months. [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Rubber-washer (compression) faucet [00:00]
- **Idea:** Press a rubber washer onto the opening.
- **Why it works:** Soft rubber squeezes into the opening and seals it.
- **But:** Every closing crushes it; it wears fast and leaks within months [00:02]. - `wear-or-leak`
- **So:** Open and close by turning a ball instead of crushing a washer [00:09]. - `reshape`
- **Shown as:** Cross-section with gold-coloured cut faces; the washer squashes, then droplets escape past it.
![Washer leaking](frames/faucet/f003_0-00-08.jpg)

### 2. Ball valve with one hole [00:10]
- **Idea:** Turn the ball to line up its hole and water flows; turn it away and it stops [00:12].
- **Why it works:** Alignment sets the flow; nothing is squeezed.
- **But:** Hot and cold still come out separately, so temperature is hard to control [00:16]. - `control`
- **So:** Drill more holes and bring hot and cold into one ball [00:20]. - `combine-layer`
- **Shown as:** A cutaway pipe on the wooden desk with a chrome ball turning inside.
![Ball valve cutaway](frames/faucet/f004_0-00-15.jpg)

### 3. Mixing ball with one lever [00:20]
- **Idea:** One ball, several holes; one small lever turn sets the temperature [00:24].
- **Why it works:** Turning the ball changes how much of each inlet lines up.
- **But:** The ball must rotate, so it never sits flush; a small gap always remains [00:27]. - `wear-or-leak`
- **So:** Seal the gap with a valve seat [00:32]. - `constrain`
- **Shown as:** Ball with red and teal inlets under the lever, then an extreme close-up of the gap.
![Mixing ball with hot and cold inlets](frames/faucet/f006_0-00-26.jpg)

### 4. Ball pressed against valve seats [00:32]
- **Idea:** Seat rings pressed against the ball close the gap.
- **Why it works:** The seat hugs the curve where the ball cannot.
- **But:** Rubbing against the ball, the seat wears down too [00:35]. - `wear-or-leak`
- **So:** Abandon the curve [00:39]: flat faces meet flush, so no soft seat is needed. - `reshape`
- **Shown as:** Grey exploded view, seat rings floating out of the ball.
![Valve seats on the ball](frames/faucet/f009_0-00-38.jpg)

### Final: Ceramic disc cartridge [00:40]
- **Idea:** Two flat, hard ceramic discs carry the water [00:40]; sliding opens the holes [00:45], turning mixes hot with cold [00:47].
- **Why it solves the chain:** Flat discs meet flush, so no gap and no seat (rungs 3-4); hard against hard does not wear (rungs 1, 4) [00:50]; one lever still sets flow and temperature (rungs 2-3).
- **Reveal:** The engineer appears at "Instead of a ball" [00:40]; closing line "A century of dripping finally stopped" [00:52].
![Ceramic discs with hot and cold arrows](frames/faucet/f011_0-00-47.jpg)

## Scar map
Every part of the final design and the failure that forced it.
| Part of the final design | Exists because of | Rung |
|---|---|---|
| Hard ceramic, not rubber | crushed washer, worn seat | 1, 4 |
| Sliding holes, no squeezing | washer crushed on every closing | 1 |
| Hot and cold in one moving part | separate supplies, poor temperature control | 2 |
| Two-motion lever (slide = flow, turn = mix) | one-turn temperature setting | 3 |
| Flat, flush faces; no soft seat | ball's gap, then seat wear | 3, 4 |

## Accuracy notes
- The real lineage is not one line: single-handle mixing cartridges (Al Moen, 1940s) and the washerless ball faucet (Landis Perkins, sold as Delta, 1950s) coexisted with compression taps; ceramic discs spread later. The one-hole ball is really an industrial quarter-turn valve, not a household stage.
- "Nothing wears down" overstates it: ceramic discs last far longer but grit can scratch or crack them, and the cartridge still has rubber seals.
