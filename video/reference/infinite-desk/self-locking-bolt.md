# Wedge-locking washers: three friction add-ons all lose to vibration, so the last design turns the loosening motion against itself

**Chain at a glance:** Plain tightened nut -> Two nuts jammed together -> Split spring washer -> Nylon-insert nut -> Interlocking-ramp washer pair

**The simple problem:** However tight a nut is, vibration eventually loosens it. Why? [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Plain nut, tightened hard [00:04]
- **Idea:** Tighten until the bolt stretches slightly.
- **Why it works:** The stretched bolt pulls like a spring; its tension clamps the parts and friction holds the nut.
- **But:** Sideways vibration makes the contact surfaces slip for an instant, turning the nut a little each time [00:12]. - `wear-or-leak`
- **So:** Add a second nut tightened against the first. - `combine-layer`
- **Shown as:** White arrows pull two plates together; a macro of the thread flanks shows the brief slip.
![Thread flanks slipping](frames/self-locking-bolt/f004_0-00-15.jpg)

### 2. Double nut [00:21]
- **Idea:** "What if we add another nut?"
- **Why it works:** Two nuts pushed against each other grip the threads hard.
- **But:** Strong vibration beats that friction and both nuts turn off together [00:25]. - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Put a spring under the nut that keeps pushing. - `constrain`
- **Shown as:** Opposing white arrows squeeze the nuts; the character then eyes the loosened pair.
![Two nuts squeezed by opposing arrows](frames/self-locking-bolt/f007_0-00-26.jpg)

### 3. Split spring washer [00:30]
- **Idea:** A split, twisted spring washer under the nut.
- **Why it works:** Compressed, it keeps pressing on the nut.
- **But:** Not strong enough for severe vibration [00:36]. - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Change the thread contact: a grippy plastic inside the nut. - `substitute-medium`
- **Shown as:** A white arrow from the washer presses on the nut face.
![Spring washer pushing on the nut](frames/self-locking-bolt/f010_0-00-37.jpg)

### 4. Nylon-insert nut [00:41]
- **Idea:** A nylon ring inside the nut.
- **Why it works:** The nylon presses into the threads, adding friction.
- **But:** Only more resistance; rotation is not fully prevented [00:47]. - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Stop adding friction; make loosening demand work the joint cannot do. - `self-powered`
- **Shown as:** Nylon colour-coded bright blue, filling the thread grooves.
![Blue nylon in the threads](frames/self-locking-bolt/f012_0-00-45.jpg)

### Final: Interlocking-ramp washer pair [00:51]
- **Idea:** Two washers with matching ramps between them and teeth outside that bite into the plate and the nut.
- **Why it solves the chain:** The teeth stop rung 1's surface slip, so turning would require the upper washer to climb the ramps and stretch the bolt further, which blocks it [00:56]. Rungs 2-4 added friction; this adds geometry.
- **Reveal:** No name is given; the closing line says the loosening motion becomes "a force that locks it even tighter" [01:05].
![Washer pair edge-on: teeth and ramp seam](frames/self-locking-bolt/f014_0-00-55.jpg)

## Accuracy notes
- Omitted key condition: the ramp angle must exceed the thread's helix angle. Real product: Nord-Lock wedge-locking washers.
- In Junker vibration tests split spring washers give almost no locking, while a correctly fitted double nut does better than shown. The order is a teaching sequence, not history.
- Nylon-insert (prevailing-torque) nuts mainly stop a nut falling off after preload is lost, consistent with the video.
