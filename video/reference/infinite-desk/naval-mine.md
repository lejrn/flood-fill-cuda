# Naval mine: first remove the human, then outwit an enemy who adapts

**Chain at a glance:** Drifting gunpowder barrel -> Anchored, wire-fired mine -> Horned contact mine -> Magnetic sensor mine -> Microphone (acoustic) mine -> Magnetic + sound + pressure mine

**The simple problem:** Make a gunpowder charge go off against an enemy ship's hull. [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Drifting gunpowder barrel [00:00]
- **Idea:** Float powder barrels down rivers into enemy ships [00:03].
- **Why it works:** The current delivers the charge for free.
- **But:** The current carried most of them the wrong way [00:06]. - `control`
- **So:** Hang a heavy anchor under the barrel. - `constrain`
- **Shown as:** A cutaway barrel full of black powder, then a barrel bobbing beside a model sailing ship on a desk-scale water tank.
![Barrel floating toward a ship](frames/naval-mine/f002_0-00-05.jpg)

### 2. Anchored, wire-fired mine [00:08]
- **Idea:** When a ship passes over, an operator sends electricity down a wire [00:12].
- **Why it works:** Fixed position, human timing.
- **But:** Someone had to wait until a ship arrived [00:17]. - `manual-effort`
- **So:** Add horns so the ship triggers it. - `automate`
- **Shown as:** A blue spark races down the wire into a cutaway mine; the character's hand rests on the dock as a ship approaches.
![Spark down the firing wire](frames/naval-mine/f004_0-00-15.jpg)

### 3. Horned contact mine [00:19]
- **Idea:** A ship bends a lead horn; its glass tube of electrolyte activates a battery that fires the mine [00:22].
- **Why it works:** The ship's own impact closes the circuit.
- **But:** It explodes only on a direct hit [00:29]. - `other:range`
- **So:** Remove the horns; add a magnetic-field sensor [00:32]. - `sense-and-react`
- **Shown as:** Cutaway horn with a red-filled tube, then a red hull gliding past the horn tips.
![Cutaway of a lead horn](frames/naval-mine/f007_0-00-25.jpg)

### 4. Magnetic sensor mine [00:32]
- **Idea:** A passing steel warship changes the magnetic field; the sensor fires the detonator [00:37].
- **Why it works:** Steel disturbs the field at a distance; no contact needed.
- **But:** Warships fitted degaussing coils making an opposite field [00:43]. - `adversary`
- **So:** Add underwater microphones [00:50]. - `sense-and-react`
- **Shown as:** White field lines bend around a grey warship; then coils wrap the hull and the lines flatten.
![Field lines bending around the warship](frames/naval-mine/f010_0-00-39.jpg)

### 5. Microphone (acoustic) mine [00:50]
- **Idea:** Microphones turn engine and propeller noise into a firing signal [00:52].
- **Why it works:** A ship can't easily silence itself.
- **But:** Imitation sounds fool it easily [00:58]. - `adversary`
- **So:** Also add pressure sensors and require all signals together. - `combine-layer`
- **Shown as:** A mine on the seabed under a red hull, then a decoy emitting blue sound rings nearby.
![Decoy sound near the mine](frames/naval-mine/f016_0-01-02.jpg)

### Final: Multi-influence mine [01:02]
- **Idea:** Pressure sensors read changes in water pressure [01:02]; modern mines check magnetism, sound and pressure together [01:07].
- **Why it solves the chain:** Anchor and self-triggering fixed 1-3; an enemy can cancel one signal (4) or fake another (5), but not easily all three.
- **Reveal:** No special name; the line ending "together" plays over a cutaway pressure plate on springs pushed by a down arrow.
![Pressure sensor cutaway](frames/naval-mine/f017_0-01-07.jpg)

## Accuracy notes
- History is compressed: drifting kegs (1777-78), shore-fired electric mines (19th century), the German "Hertz horn" (late 1860s), magnetic mines (1918, mass use 1939), then degaussing, acoustic (1940) and pressure mines (1944).
- Contact mines were never retired, and influence mines usually lie on the seabed, unnamed here.
- Pressure sensors mainly sense the pressure drop under a moving hull; real mines add ship counters and arming delays.
