# ABS: each fix removes one limit and exposes the next - force, then heat, then wheel lock

**Chain at a glance:** Foot-powered drum brake -> Hydraulic drum brake -> Hydraulic disc brake -> Sensor-pulsed anti-lock brake

**The simple problem:** A spinning wheel must be stopped, and at first the only power is the driver's foot. [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Foot-powered drum brake [00:00]
- **Idea:** Pedal force spreads brake shoes against a drum turning with the wheel [00:03].
- **Why it works:** Shoe-on-drum friction slows the tyre [00:08].
- **But:** As cars got faster, foot strength alone could not stop them [00:11]. - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Use oil: a small piston drives a larger one. - `amplify-trade`
- **Shown as:** The character sits behind a toy pedal pressed by a dress shoe; a front cutaway shows shoes and drum; then the shoe-on-pedal beside a bare tyre at the "human strength" line.
![Foot on the pedal beside the wheel](frames/car-brakes-abs/f004_0-00-15.jpg)

### 2. Hydraulic drum brake [00:15]
- **Idea:** The pedal compresses oil in a small piston; the pressure moves a larger piston that pushes the shoes [00:17].
- **Why it works:** Same pressure on a bigger piston gives a much bigger force [00:21].
- **But:** Continuous braking makes huge heat, the closed drum traps it, and braking power drops [00:29]. - `side-effect`
- **So:** Move the friction to an open disc squeezed by pads. - `reshape`
- **Shown as:** A transparent master cylinder of amber oil in front of the character; heat as a red glow along the drum's rubbing surface.
![Drum glowing red with heat](frames/car-brakes-abs/f009_0-00-33.jpg)

### 3. Disc brake [00:41]
- **Idea:** A disc fixed to the wheel, pinched by pads.
- **Why it works:** Exposed to air, the disc sheds heat easily [00:46].
- **But:** Hard braking stops the wheel completely; it skids and the car cannot be controlled [00:52]. - `control`
- **So:** Watch each wheel and ease pressure just before it locks. - `sense-and-react`
- **Shown as:** Ventilated disc and caliper on the desk; the character's face fills the frame at "another problem emerged"; the wheel then slides along the desk, leaving a dark streak.
![Locked wheel skidding along the desk](frames/car-brakes-abs/f015_0-00-58.jpg)

### Final: Anti-lock brakes [00:59]
- **Idea:** Wheel sensors spot an impending lock; pressure is briefly released and reapplied once the wheel turns, repeated very rapidly [01:02].
- **Why it solves the chain:** It keeps the hydraulic force and the cool open disc but caps the force where the tyre still grips, so the car stops and stays steerable.
- **Reveal:** Never named; the video ends on cars stopping safely [01:09], leaving "ABS" to the viewer.
![Sensor beam aimed at the toothed ring](frames/car-brakes-abs/f018_0-01-09.jpg)

## Scar map
Every part of the final design and the failure that forced it.
| Part of the final design | Exists because of | Rung |
|---|---|---|
| Hydraulic small and large pistons | Foot force too weak for faster cars | 1 |
| Open disc and pads, not a closed drum | Heat trapped in the drum, braking fading | 2 |
| Wheel sensor with toothed ring | Hard braking locks the wheel | 3 |
| Rapid release-and-reapply of pressure | A locked wheel skids and cannot be steered | 3 |

- **Storyteller:** "You" press the pedal; an anonymous "they" makes every fix. Connectives: But / So they / But there was a problem / But another problem emerged.
- **Visual devices:** Toy-scale wheel, pedal and brake parts on the wooden desk (one shot has an outdoor fence-and-trees backdrop); drum cutaways; transparent cylinders with amber oil; red glow = heat; character close-up at the problem line; skid streak on the wood; sensor drawn with a red beam.
- **Pacing:** 4 rungs in 01:14, about 18 s each; the hydraulic rung is longest (~26 s) since it carries a fix and a new failure.

## Accuracy notes
- Real wheel-speed sensors are magnetic (inductive or Hall-effect); the red beam is artistic. The hydraulic modulator (valves and pump) is not shown.
- History compressed: hydraulic brakes (1920s), car disc brakes (1950s), electronic ABS (Bosch, 1978); vacuum boosters, another answer to the force problem, are skipped.
- ABS mainly preserves steering; it does not always shorten stopping distance (e.g. loose gravel, snow).
