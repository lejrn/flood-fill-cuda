# Diesel hammer: each fix removes one outside helper (crew, rope, boiler, igniter) until the falling ram powers its own next lift

**Chain at a glance:** Rock dropped on pile -> Rock between guide posts -> Rope wound on drum -> Steam-lifted hammer in cylinder -> Fuel blast with igniter -> Self-igniting diesel hammer

**The simple problem:** A pile must be pounded into the ground by lifting something heavy and dropping it, over and over. [00:00]

## The ladder

### 1. Drop a rock on it [00:02]
- **Idea:** Hoist a huge rock over the pile and let it fall.
- **Why it works:** A heavy falling mass hits hard.
- **But:** After one hit the rock tumbles off to the side [00:05] - `control`
- **So:** Posts on both sides let it move only between them [00:07] - `constrain`
- **Shown as:** A toy stake under a tripod with a rock on a rope; the tumble is told, not shown.
![Rock hung over the pile from a tripod](frames/diesel-hammer/f002_0-00-04.jpg)

### 2. Guide posts [00:07]
- **Idea:** Posts (leads) keep the rock on line.
- **Why it works:** Every blow lands square on the pile.
- **But:** Hauling it up by hand every time is brutal [00:12] - `manual-effort`
- **So:** Wind the rope around a drum and crank it [00:15] - `amplify-trade`
- **Shown as:** The rock sliding between posts, then a line of tiny workers pulling the rope.
![Crew hauling the rope at the guide frame](frames/diesel-hammer/f004_0-00-15.jpg)

### 3. Rope on a drum [00:15]
- **Idea:** A turning drum winds the rope.
- **Why it works:** A long, easy turning path replaces a short, hard pull.
- **But:** Winding takes forever [00:20] - `too-weak-or-slow`
- **So:** Ditch the rope; lift with steam [00:24] - `substitute-medium`
- **Shown as:** Two horses walk a big drum around (narration says "cranked", the picture shows horse power).
![Horses turning the rope drum](frames/diesel-hammer/f005_0-00-16.jpg)

### 4. Steam hammer [00:24]
- **Idea:** Hammer inside a cylinder: steam in raises it, steam out drops it [00:27].
- **Why it works:** A valve does the lifting, nonstop and effortless [00:34].
- **But:** Every job drags a boiler and a water tank along [00:40] - `baggage`
- **So:** Put fuel inside the cylinder and set it alight [00:49] - `substitute-medium`
- **Shown as:** Cutaway cylinder filling with white steam and venting; then the engineer looms behind a boiler and a blue tank.
![Boiler and water tank dragged along](frames/diesel-hammer/f011_0-00-43.jpg)

### 5. Fuel blast with an igniter [00:49]
- **Idea:** The fuel blast shoves the hammer up with no waiting [00:53].
- **Why it works:** Energy is released right in the cylinder; no steam to make and pipe.
- **But:** An ignition device means bulky equipment again [00:58] - `baggage`
- **So:** Use the force of the falling hammer itself [01:04] - `self-powered`
- **Shown as:** Fuel spray in the cutaway, a glowing cyan spark device, then cables, battery box and cable reel clustered on the orange hammer.
![Igniter equipment bolted onto the hammer](frames/diesel-hammer/f016_0-01-04.jpg)

### Final: Diesel hammer [01:04]
- **Idea:** The falling ram compresses the air, the hot air ignites the fuel, the blast throws the ram up, and the cycle repeats.
- **Why it solves the chain:** The guide stays; the lift is internal (no crew, rope, horses), fuel burns in place (no boiler), ignition comes from the fall (no igniter). Only fuel goes in.
- **Reveal:** Last line, "This is the diesel hammer" [01:20], over the orange hammer on a crawler rig.
![Compression chamber glowing red as the fuel ignites](frames/diesel-hammer/f018_0-01-13.jpg)

## Accuracy notes
- "One engineer" merges a century: steam pile hammers date from the 1840s (James Nasmyth), the diesel pile hammer from 1930s Germany (Delmag). The igniter rung is mostly a logical bridge.
- A diesel hammer needs the rig to lift the ram for the first stroke; the blast also pushes down on the pile, adding to each blow.
- "Detonates" is loose: the fuel auto-ignites and burns fast.
